# Lessons for the next research project

19 November 2026 · Adithya Rajendran · CMPE 249, San José State University

### Measure how much room a simple rule leaves

Before building a method, run the simplest credible baseline on a small, representative set. Count what it gets right, what it gets wrong, and what it leaves unanswered. For each miss, ask whether the proposed method would receive enough information to fix it. On our 88 identical MuJoCo pairs, the images could not distinguish retention from removal. Homography already abstained on every pair. A more complex verifier could gain coverage there only by guessing. The opportunity to detect genuine removals was in other views. [Paired results](../paper/evidence/summary.json)

### Start with a runnable external baseline and real data

For the next topic, choose an external baseline with accessible weights, a usable license and a documented evaluation command. Reproduce a published result and inspect its errors on real observations before designing a simulator or commissioning labels. Our simulations let us change one object's state while holding the camera fixed. They gave us an exact ambiguity test. The STILL training code was public, but its trained verifier was unavailable for our comparison. A checkpoint and a small real dataset in hand would have changed what we could responsibly promise. [Checkpoint and comparison record](2026-11-19-still-verifier.md)

### Pre-commit one experiment and honor its outcome

Write down the population, comparison, denominator, error that matters, and the result that ends the experiment. Put the protocol in version control before collecting the evaluation data. Here dense registration found more genuine removals after camera motion in both simulators. It also introduced two false deletions, and the declared allowance was zero. That is a reportable result. A later camera angle or a threshold found after scoring answers a different question. Call it exploratory and record which images were already visible when the question arose. [Protocol](../development/paper_audit/PROTOCOL.md) · [Published comparison](../paper/Rajendran-counterfactual-scene-memory.pdf)

Before spending a week on the next idea, bring three things to the first research meeting. Show a reproducible baseline, one failure on real data that the new method could plausibly fix, and a short experiment whose outcome could change the decision to continue.
