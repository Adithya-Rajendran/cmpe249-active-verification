# Is there a paper in the scene-memory experiments?

19 November 2026. The study is ready for author review as a simulation-based audit of when a robot can delete a remembered object. The main experiment has a prospective design, a negative result under its original success rule, and an independent recount. The camera and room follow-ups establish where a striking failure recurs and where it disappears. My recommendation is to circulate the revised draft for technical feedback, then prepare it as a short empirical paper. ## What the evidence establishes

Sixteen shelf and tabletop layouts supplied 1,280 queries across Isaac Sim and MuJoCo. Dense registration improved the primary count of clear removals after camera motion from 67 to 76 of 80 in Isaac and from 63 to 78 of 80 in MuJoCo. It also introduced two new false removals on MuJoCo shelves, failing the declared improvement rule. The inferential unit is the layout. The engines render the same layouts. The cleanest test asks what the images can establish. MuJoCo supplied 88 pairs in which the reference, box, prompt and revisit were identical whether the target stayed or disappeared. Homography, the dense rule and registered Qwen made definite decisions on zero, one and four pairs. The four Qwen pairs spanned three layouts. Each call of REMOVED on both states was right once and wrong once. Isaac supplied no exact pairs on the main panel. Its physical-state errors remain measurable. Pixel differences alone do not tell us whether a person could decide the state. The blinded reader also failed the cross-simulator qualification for partly occluded removals. Leveling the shelf edge in the image stopped all four previously identified errors at registration. On eight fresh plain corridors, the primary oblique test yielded zero double-removals on eight exact pairs. The secondary frontal test yielded seven. The corrected furnished workshop used the same eight layouts and frontal cameras. All eight pairs remained exact, and registration stopped every pair. Several room features changed together. In the corrected workshop the simple homography answered every open-door query correctly and abstained on every closed one in each engine. Registered Qwen still falsely removed an exposed drill four times in Isaac and twice in MuJoCo. These are matched sensitivity results. Pooling these panels would count the same layouts repeatedly. ## What the recent literature changes

| Primary work reviewed through 19 November 2026 | Consequence for this paper |
| --- | --- |
| PBD-AG uses measured depth and a history of observable misses to update object existence. Its ablation already measures false absence. | Visibility-gated deletion and its safety tradeoff have direct precedent. Our experiment can test a single RGB revisit by changing the hidden physical state while holding the verifier's complete input fixed. [Bao et al., §3.3 and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10449v2) |
| STILL learns to verify an object's registered location and improves multiview change detection. | Our zero-shot VGGT–Qwen system uses a different registration, reader training and score fusion. Its errors say nothing direct about the published STILL system. [Xie et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18734v1) |
| TRAPSBench measures abstention on matched answerable and unanswerable physics questions. The Counterfactual Memory Audit changes history at an identical present to test robot actions. | Both matched ambiguity tests and identical-observation memory audits have precedents. Here the earlier image and task also stay fixed. Two hidden states give opposite physical truth for the same deletion decision. [TRAPSBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13167v1) · [Counterfactual Memory Audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27247) |
| SafeVantage selects reachable views for category-presence decisions. VA-TAMP inserts sensing into a partially observed manipulation plan. | A new claim about active perception needs to beat a credible visibility-based choice at matched movement cost. [SafeVantage](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36906v2) · [VA-TAMP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16213) |
| ReScene, revised 17 November, recovers a scene's chronology from sparse revisits and evaluates reconstruction and storage. | A current system can preserve or revise a 3D scene across visits. ## Publication decision

The subsequent [versioned release](https://github.com/Adithya-Rajendran/cmpe249-active-verification/releases/tag/scene-memory-audit-v1) provides the revised paper, exact images, score tables, and independent verification instructions.

1. **Circulate the audit.** The local revision includes the shared-registration comparison and all three sensitivity tests. A fresh extraction of the supplement reproduced the primary results and the text of all nine PDF pages. The versioned release now includes the exact RGB inputs, native captures, decisions and verification instructions. Readers can verify the downloads against the checksums in the tagged repository. Select a venue and check its page and artifact rules when preparing that version. [Evidence guide](../paper/README.md) · [Delivered-image archive check](../paper/evidence/rgb-archive-check.json) · [Input restoration check](../paper/evidence/packet-restore-check.json)
2. **If one more comparison is affordable, test the closest external reader first.** Use the authors' trained STILL verifier on the saved reference-and-revisit pairs if its weights and two-view interface can be reproduced. Record the version, allowed inputs and mapping from its unseen response to abstention before scoring. Report clear removals, false deletions, abstentions and exact-pair commitments. Treat this as an additional comparator on an inspected panel. A broader method ranking needs fresh layouts. [STILL implementation and training instructions](https://github.com/Oran9er/STILL)
3. **Reserve active sensing for a separate claim.** Use fresh layouts with balanced retained and removed states. Include places that one reachable view can reveal and places that remain hidden. Compare a chosen view with a fixed view, a random feasible view and a visibility-based choice, giving each the same time, travel and image budget. Retain the initial-view result as a reference. Freeze a useful gain and an acceptable added false-removal bound before collection. If the selected view cannot beat the strongest fixed or visibility-based choice within those bounds, stop the active-method claim and publish the audit on its own. The simulation-only scope fits the current question. [Study design](../paper/evidence/design/PROTOCOL.md) · [Workshop feasibility checks](2026-11-18-workshop-clearance.md)
